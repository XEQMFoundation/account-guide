XEQM is not sold by the project. It is bought on a market, and the project's own FAQ names a single exchange where it trades. Everything else  -  a presale, a broker, a private sale, a bot that will "fill" an order  -  is somebody's offer rather than a venue, and the difference decides whether you end up holding a coin or a promise.

## The one venue the project names

The project's FAQ names one exchange as the place XEQM trades. That is the whole of the official answer, and anything beyond it is a claim made by the venue rather than by the project.

If a page lists a dozen markets for the coin, ask which of them the project names. The list will be shorter than the page.

## Why there is no official sale

A network that launched through a migration does not need a presale, and this one did not run one. The coins that exist came from the migration and from the block reward.

That matters because "official presale" is one of the most effective phrases in this space. There is nothing for it to sell.

## Buying is custody until you withdraw

A balance on an exchange is a claim on the exchange, not coins in your wallet. The withdrawal is the step that makes them yours, and it is the step people postpone.

Postponing it is a decision to trust a third party with the balance. [Withdraw XEQM from an exchange](https://github.com/XEQMFoundation/account-guide/blob/main/how-to-withdraw-xeqm-from-exchange.md) covers the move.

## What to check before you deposit anything

That the venue is the one the project names, that the market is XEQM rather than a wrapped asset with a similar ticker, and that the withdrawal route to your own wallet works.

A test withdrawal of a small amount answers the third question before a larger balance is at stake.

## Wrapped XEQM is not XEQM

A market for WXEQ is a market for a representation on another chain. Buying it gives you that asset, with its own risks, and it cannot be staked without unwrapping.

The ticker has a history too: a wrapped version of the predecessor coin used the same name. Check the chain, not the ticker. [What WXEQ is on XEQM](https://github.com/XEQMFoundation/custody-and-protection/blob/main/what-is-wxeq-on-xeqm-network.md) covers the distinction.

## Why a thin market behaves differently

With one venue and modest volume, an order can move the price and a spread can be wide. That is a property of a small market rather than a fault in the coin.

A buyer who assumes the liquidity of a large exchange will be surprised by both the spread and the time a withdrawal takes to be processed.

## What to do between buying and withdrawing

Withdraw promptly. A balance left on a venue is exposure to that venue's solvency, its withdrawal policy and its uptime, none of which you control.

The small test withdrawal first, then the rest, is the order that keeps a mistake cheap. [Withdraw XEQM from an exchange](https://github.com/XEQMFoundation/account-guide/blob/main/how-to-withdraw-xeqm-from-exchange.md) covers it.

## What a buyer should not do

Do not send funds to an individual who promises to buy on your behalf, do not accept a "better rate" in a direct message, and do not deposit into anything described as a presale.

Each of those asks you to hand over value before value is delivered, which is the definition of the risk a venue exists to remove.

## What to record

Keep the purchase record, the withdrawal identifier and the amount. Those three let you explain the balance later without reconstructing a conversation.

Store them with your other records rather than in the exchange's own history, which you may lose access to.

## Why no price appears here

A price is a market observation that changes continuously, and this project does not publish one. A figure in a guide would be wrong before the page finished loading.

What can be described is the market structure: one named venue, a wrapped alternative elsewhere, and no official seller.

## Peer-to-peer trades

Buying from an individual avoids an exchange and adds a counterparty. There is no escrow unless the platform provides one, and a payment made first is a payment made on trust.

If you use a peer-to-peer arrangement, treat the other side as a stranger with an incentive, and keep the transaction identifiers for both legs.

## What a scam looks like here

A presale that takes deposits and delivers nothing. A broker who will "buy for you" if you send funds first. A bot that promises to fill an order at a better price. A support account that will "release" a stuck trade for a fee.

All of them want value before value is delivered, which is the one thing a real venue does not ask for. [How to spot XEQM phishing](https://github.com/XEQMFoundation/custody-and-protection/blob/main/how-to-spot-xeqm-phishing.md) covers the patterns.

## What to do with what you buy

Withdraw to your own wallet, confirm the balance after the rescan, and write the seed down if you have not already. A purchase is not complete until the coins are somewhere you control.

Then keep the withdrawal record. It is the document that explains how the balance arrived. [How to back up XEQM WALLET](https://github.com/XEQMFoundation/custody-and-protection/blob/main/how-to-back-up-xeqm-wallet.md) covers the backup.

## What about mining or earning it

There is no mining on this network: it runs on proof of stake, and coins enter through the block reward to service nodes and the foundation.

Earning therefore means operating a node or contributing to a pool, both of which require a bond rather than a purchase. [How much XEQM runs a service node](https://github.com/XEQMFoundation/account-guide/blob/main/how-much-xeqm-to-run-a-service-node.md) covers the entry.

## What a new buyer should accept

That the market is thin, the venue is singular, and the asset is not sold by the project. Those three facts describe a small network rather than a hidden opportunity.

A buyer who understands them can proceed carefully. One who does not is the audience these offers are written for.

## What a first purchase should look like

A small amount, withdrawn immediately, confirmed in your own wallet. That sequence tests the venue, the withdrawal route and your backup for the price of a fee.

The mistake people make is buying a large amount and leaving it on the venue while they decide what to do next. Decide first.

## What the summary should not promise

Nothing about price, liquidity or future listings. A guide that predicts a market is guessing with somebody else's money.

What this page can say is where the project points and how to move what you buy into your own control.

## What to ask before trusting a venue

Does the project name it, can you withdraw to your own wallet, and does a small withdrawal arrive? Three questions, each answerable before real money is involved.

A venue that fails any of them is not a place to buy, whatever its interface offers.

## The summary

One named venue, no official sale, no price quoted here, and a wrapped asset that is not the coin. Buy, withdraw, verify, and keep the record.

That is the whole of the practical answer. [Which wallets support XEQM](https://github.com/XEQMFoundation/account-guide/blob/main/which-wallets-support-xeqm-coin.md) covers where it can be held afterwards.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I buy XEQM directly from the project?

No. The project does not sell the coin; it is bought on a market.

### Which exchange trades XEQM?

One is named by the project's own FAQ. Treat any other venue as an unverified claim about itself.

### Is there a presale?

No. The coins came from the migration and the block reward.

### Can I buy WXEQ instead?

You can buy the wrapped asset, and it is not the native coin. It cannot be staked and it is visible on the chain where it lives.
